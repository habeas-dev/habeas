How to download your ING transactions and statements

ING lets you view your transactions and download monthly statements, but one account and one month at a time. Pulling a whole year, or several accounts together, is repetitive manual work.

What you get

Habeas is an open-source browser extension that collects your documents from ING and saves them wherever you choose. From this source it extracts:

  • movimientos — each movement with its date, amount, currency, description and counterparty.
  • informe · PDF or Excel — each document with its date, amount, currency and numbering.
  • extracto-mensual — each document with its date, amount, currency and numbering.
Worth knowing: ING serves a limited history — around 90 days — and enforces it strictly on current accounts, so Habeas brings that window rather than years back: the sooner you start, the more you keep. If you hold several accounts, it lets you choose which ones the export covers and remembers that choice between syncs.
What it does not cover: this source does not extract inversión. It is stated here so you know what to expect before installing anything.

How it works

It is not a service that connects to ingdirect.es on its own. It runs inside your own browser, reusing the session you already have open:

  1. You go to ingdirect.es and sign in yourself, as always, with your MFA or whatever code you use.
  2. Habeas reuses that already-authenticated session to request your documents, exactly as the site itself does.
  3. You choose the destination: a download, a local folder, your Google Drive, Dropbox, WebDAV, S3 or an endpoint of your own.

Your password is never read, never stored and never sent anywhere, and nothing runs in the background while you are away. This is an audited source, verified by the project against the real service.

Install on Chrome Install on Firefox

Free and open source (AGPL-3.0). Install the extension and enable the “ING España” source from Settings → Browse sources.

Chrome will warn you that the extension is not trusted. That happens to any developer account recently registered on the Chrome Web Store, it fades after a few months, and it says nothing about the code — which you can read in full.

Frequently asked questions

Does Habeas store my ING password?

No. You sign in yourself, on the usual website, including any verification code. Habeas reuses the session your browser already has open and never reads or stores your credentials.

Is my data sent to a server?

No. Everything happens inside your browser. Documents only go to the destination you pick: a download, a folder on your machine, your own Google Drive or Dropbox, or an endpoint you configure.

What format do I get the documents in?

The original documents exactly as the service issues them (PDF, Excel), together with a manifest holding the normalised data.

Is it free?

Yes. Habeas is free software under the AGPL-3.0 licence and its code is public on GitHub.

Your service is not on the list?

Habeas can learn a new one without any code. Record mode watches the site’s own API while you browse it normally and drafts the source for you; you test it against your own account, and then keep it to yourself or share it so everyone gets it. That is where most of the catalog came from.
