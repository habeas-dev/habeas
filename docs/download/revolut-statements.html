How to download your Revolut transactions and statements

Revolut offers statements as PDF or CSV, one period at a time. Building a continuous history, or keeping it alongside your other accounts, still means going back every month and downloading by hand.

What you get

Habeas is an open-source browser extension that collects your documents from Revolut and saves them wherever you choose. From this source it extracts:

  • transactions — each movement with its date, amount, currency, description and counterparty.
  • extracto · PDF or CSV — each document with its date, amount, currency and numbering.

How it works

It is not a service that connects to revolut.com on its own. It runs inside your own browser, reusing the session you already have open:

  1. You go to revolut.com and sign in yourself, as always, with your MFA or whatever code you use.
  2. Habeas reuses that already-authenticated session to request your documents, exactly as the site itself does.
  3. You choose the destination: a download, a local folder, your Google Drive, Dropbox, WebDAV, S3 or an endpoint of your own.

Your password is never read, never stored and never sent anywhere, and nothing runs in the background while you are away. This is a community source: its definition is public and reviewable, and it runs under the same security rules as every other.

Install on Chrome Install on Firefox

Free and open source (AGPL-3.0). Install the extension and enable the “Revolut” source from Settings → Browse sources.

Chrome will warn you that the extension is not trusted. That happens to any developer account recently registered on the Chrome Web Store, it fades after a few months, and it says nothing about the code — which you can read in full.

Frequently asked questions

Does Habeas store my Revolut password?

No. You sign in yourself, on the usual website, including any verification code. Habeas reuses the session your browser already has open and never reads or stores your credentials.

Is my data sent to a server?

No. Everything happens inside your browser. Documents only go to the destination you pick: a download, a folder on your machine, your own Google Drive or Dropbox, or an endpoint you configure.

What format do I get the documents in?

The original documents exactly as the service issues them (PDF, CSV), together with a manifest holding the normalised data.

Is it free?

Yes. Habeas is free software under the AGPL-3.0 licence and its code is public on GitHub.

What has changed in this source

This source is a public definition, and every change to it is on the record. Most recent first:

  • 2026-08-26 — Documents a known limit rather than fixing it: weekend foreign-currency card payments arrive about 1% short, because Revolut’s out-of-hours surcharge leaves the balance but never appears in the data. 1.48 EUR across 3 of 167 movements, and every way of correcting it costs more than it recovers.
  • 2026-08-26 — Drops card verifications (0 EUR authorisations, flagged cardVerification) — they move no money and are not movements. Fixes vault transfers: Revolut books them from the paying side, so money returning from a savings vault arrived negative, and the balance they carry belongs to the vault rather than to the personal account. Reported against 167 real movements, of which 151 of 166 balance links already chained to the cent. Also drops anything whose state is not COMPLETED (5 DECLINED + 1 REVERTED in the sample were retried charges whose successful twin also arrived).
  • 2026-08-26 — Records authorship: the session was recorded and the definition written by the maintainer. No change to extraction.
  • 2026-08-12 — Automatic sync no longer starts until app.revolut.com/home has finished loading. The app fires authenticated requests on earlier screens, so a sync could begin before the view holding the data existed.
  • 2026-08-08 — Adds `content` (bilingual copy for the guide page on habeas.dev) and `brand`.

Your service is not on the list?

Habeas can learn a new one without any code. Record mode watches the site’s own API while you browse it normally and drafts the source for you; you test it against your own account, and then keep it to yourself or share it so everyone gets it. That is where most of the catalog came from.
