How to download your Raisin transactions, deposits and documents

Raisin spreads what matters across several products: the account movements, the fixed-term deposits, the savings balance and the documents for each. Each lives on its own screen, so the full picture is never in one place.

What you get

Habeas is an open-source browser extension that collects your documents from Raisin and saves them wherever you choose. From this source it extracts:

  • movimientos — each movement with its date, amount, currency, description and counterparty.
  • depositos — each position with its date, amount, currency and instrument.
  • ahorro — each movement with its date, amount, currency, description and counterparty.
  • documentos — each document with its date, amount, currency and numbering.
  • documentos-ahorro — each document with its date, amount, currency and numbering.

How it works

It is not a service that connects to raisin.com on its own. It runs inside your own browser, reusing the session you already have open:

  1. You go to raisin.com and sign in yourself, as always, with your MFA or whatever code you use.
  2. Habeas reuses that already-authenticated session to request your documents, exactly as the site itself does.
  3. You choose the destination: a download, a local folder, your Google Drive, Dropbox, WebDAV, S3 or an endpoint of your own.

Your password is never read, never stored and never sent anywhere, and nothing runs in the background while you are away. This is an audited source, verified by the project against the real service.

Install on Chrome Install on Firefox

Free and open source (AGPL-3.0). Install the extension and enable the “Raisin” source from Settings → Browse sources.

Chrome will warn you that the extension is not trusted. That happens to any developer account recently registered on the Chrome Web Store, it fades after a few months, and it says nothing about the code — which you can read in full.

Frequently asked questions

Does Habeas store my Raisin password?

No. You sign in yourself, on the usual website, including any verification code. Habeas reuses the session your browser already has open and never reads or stores your credentials.

Is my data sent to a server?

No. Everything happens inside your browser. Documents only go to the destination you pick: a download, a folder on your machine, your own Google Drive or Dropbox, or an endpoint you configure.

What format do I get the documents in?

The normalised data in a manifest, along with whatever original documents the service offers.

Is it free?

Yes. Habeas is free software under the AGPL-3.0 licence and its code is public on GitHub.
